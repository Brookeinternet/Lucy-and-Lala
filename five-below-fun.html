The doors opened—and the treasure hunt began

Lucy and Lala walked into Five Below with matching determined faces and a simple mission: look at everything before choosing anything. That plan lasted until the first wall of plushies came into view. The girls hurried forward, stopped in the middle of the aisle, and stared up at rows of bright animals, pastel creatures, and silly faces as if they had discovered a candy-colored museum.

Lucy became the official shelf inspector. She pointed out every pink plushie she could find and whispered “this one is my baby” to at least four of them. Lala was drawn to the funniest toys—the ones with glitter, enormous eyes, or expressions that looked ready to tell a joke. Their excitement bounced back and forth until even walking to the next aisle became its own little parade.

Our one-special-thing challenge

Before shopping, we gave each girl a small budget and explained that she could choose one special item. We did not rush the decision. Lucy and Lala could hold a toy, inspect it, return it carefully, and change their minds. That turned an overwhelming aisle into a toddler-sized lesson about choices.

  1. We looked through the entire plush section first.
  2. Each twin picked two favorites for a final comparison.
  3. We asked which toy they would still want at home tomorrow.
  4. The unchosen toys were returned to their shelves together.
  5. At checkout, each girl carried her own final choice.
Parent note: Store inventory and prices change. Set the spending limit before entering, supervise children closely, and check age labels and small parts before purchase.

The glitter poop emoji incident 💩

Then Lala spotted it: a glittery poop emoji plushie sitting behind the other toys. She gasped so loudly that Lucy spun around to see what had happened. Lala held it up with both hands and dissolved into giggles. Lucy tried to remain serious, but the sparkly face was impossible to ignore. Soon both girls were laughing in the aisle and politely introducing the plushie to every pink animal nearby.

For several minutes it became the star of an improvised show. It hid behind the shelf, “surprised” the other toys, and traveled toward the next aisle under Lala’s arm. The silly plushie was not the most elegant discovery of the day, but it was the one that created the biggest memory.

Pink plushie headquarters

Lucy’s favorites were soft, pink, and extremely huggable. She compared ears, paws, and tiny embroidered faces with the concentration of a collector. Lala helped by squeezing each one and declaring whether it was “nap soft.” Together they created an imaginary plushie family before carefully returning most of the members to their shelf.

This slower style of shopping worked beautifully. Instead of hearing “no” again and again, the girls heard, “You may enjoy looking, and then you will choose.” They practiced carrying toys gently, leaving packages closed, and making room for other shoppers. The aisle stayed playful without becoming chaotic.

The final picks and the proud walk to checkout

When it was time to decide, Lucy chose the cuddly pink favorite she had returned to twice. Lala stayed loyal to the glittery silly plushie that had made everyone laugh. Each twin held her treasure close while we walked past the last toy display. They were tempted, of course, but the girls remembered that choosing one special thing meant leaving the rest for another day.

At the register, they placed their toys on the counter, waited for the scan, and said thank you. The ride home was quieter: Lucy stroked her plushie’s ears while Lala made hers peek over the car-seat buckle. By bedtime, both toys had names, voices, and assigned sleeping spots. A quick store stop had become a full Lucy & Lala adventure.

Make your own toy-aisle treasure hunt

  • Find one toy in each rainbow color.
  • Choose the softest-looking plushie without touching every toy.
  • Spot three funny faces and invent their names.
  • Take a photo of favorites, then select one within the budget.
